What if you cook a turkey with the giblets inside?

It depends on the bag. Paper wrapped: no concern, if the turkey is fully cooked. Plastic, and the bag melted or changed: do not eat the giblets or the turkey. Plastic untouched: safe to use.

If the bag is paper

If the bag is paper

If the giblets were wrapped in paper, there is no concern, as long as the turkey is fully cooked to 165 F, measured with a food thermometer. Take the bag out now. The done check on the bird itself: A meat thermometer: 165 F in the thickest part of the breast, the innermost thigh, and the innermost wing. Color is not a reliable signal. If all three read 165 F, dinner goes ahead.

If the bag is plastic

If the bag is plastic

If giblets were packed in a plastic bag, and the bag has been altered or melted by the cooking process, do not use the giblets or the poultry because harmful chemicals may have leached into the surrounding meat. If the plastic bag was not altered, the giblets and poultry should be safe to use. Melted, warped, or stuck to the meat means do not use it. An unchanged bag is the soft answer; the 165 F check is the proof.

Caught it while the bird is still cooking

Caught it while the bird is still cooking

On a frozen bird, pull the packet out with tongs after 20 to 30 minutes, once the meat softens. For a thawed bird not yet in the oven: Remove the giblets from the turkey cavities after thawing. Cook separately. Check both cavities: the neck often rides in one, the bag in the other.

USDA's third safe way is the microwave: follow your microwave manual for size and timing, and cook the turkey right after it thaws. Never thaw a turkey on the counter. The outside warms up hours before the inside thaws. Bacteria grow in that warm layer.
