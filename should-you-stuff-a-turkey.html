Should you stuff a turkey?

No, not for safety. For optimum safety, stuffing a turkey is not recommended. For more even cooking, it is recommended you cook your stuffing outside the bird in a casserole.

Why the outside wins

Stuffing sits in the middle of the bird, the last place heat reaches. The turkey can look done while the stuffing is still cold in the center. A casserole has no such hiding place: every part reaches temperature evenly.

The one rule if it is inside

The stuffing must reach a safe minimum internal temperature of 165 F. Check the center of the stuffing with a food thermometer: 165 F in the thickest part of the breast, the innermost thigh, and the innermost wing. If the stuffing is short of 165 F, the bird keeps cooking, no matter how the meat looks.

USDA's third safe way is the microwave: follow your oven manual for size and timing, and cook the turkey right after it thaws. Never thaw a turkey on the counter. The outside warms up hours before the inside thaws. Bacteria grow in that warm layer.
